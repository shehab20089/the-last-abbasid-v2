# The Last Abbasid: agent briefing

Read this first in every session. Details live in `README.md` and `PROGRESS.md` (the state of the work and the next actions). Keep this file short and current.

## What this project is
- A 2D pixel-art action platformer in Godot 4.7.2 (typed GDScript), set during the Mongol sack of Baghdad, February 1258. The hero, Yusuf ibn Harun, is a guardsman of the Caliph. Historically grounded, fictional characters and events; no magic, no supernatural powers, no fantasy monsters, no caricatures.
- **Visual direction:** dark, detailed, semi-realistic pixel art at 640×360 with integer scaling; night over a burning city; firelight warm, everything else cool and dim; characters outlined and rim-lit so they read against the scenery. Original designs only; take qualities from Blasphemous, Dead Cells and Prince of Persia, never their art. The user's concept art pack (`docs/concept_art/`) sets the direction: black banners with a gold crescent, cranes and hanging cages, lattice balconies, cobalt tilework, square crenellated gate towers, dark lamellar with bronze rivets, round bronze shields.
- **Priorities:** combat feel and readability first, then art consistency, then content. A polished short slice beats a long unfinished one.

## Rules
- Typed GDScript everywhere. `project.godot` turns untyped declarations and unsafe property/method access, casts and call arguments into errors, so such a script fails to load. Read `Variant` values into typed variables before use (`var x: int = dict["k"]`), never pass them straight into typed calls.
- Feature folders (`features/<feature>/`), shared presentation in `shared/`, the session in `app/`. No global event bus, no universal manager, no autoloads unless truly needed.
- Controllers drive bodies from outside: `WarriorInput` drives the `Warrior`; `EnemyBrain` subclasses drive `MongolSoldier`. Bodies carry rules (health, poise, guarding, staggers); brains decide.
- Shared tuning lives in read-only Resources (`WarriorProfile`, `EnemyProfile`, `AttackDefinition`). Mutable state belongs to the actor.
- Gameplay never depends on presentation. VFX and sound are wired in `app/main.gd` from signals (`struck`, `swung`, `telegraphed`, hero and soldier signals). `VfxDirector`, `SoundDirector`, `MusicDirector` know no gameplay types.
- Attack timing is animation-driven: an attack's frames (`active_from`..`active_to`, `recovery_from`, `telegraph_frame`, `lunge_*`) index its animation strip; the hitbox each active frame is the blade's swept polygon from `FrameHitboxes`, generated with the sprites.
- Generated files are generated: `assets/**` (art, audio, fonts), `features/levels/*/[level].tscn` and `app/main.tscn`. Change the generator or the data (`tools/levels/<level>.mjs`) and rebuild.
- Soldiers: swordsman, spearman (his low sweep glints amber: jump or roll), archer, keshig veteran (a swordsman whose quick cut is followed by a backhand after a held beat), mace-bearer (`EnemyProfile.unflinching`: light blows do not stop him; his overhead blow breaks a guard), Georgian shield-bearer (`shield_wall`: his tall shield turns every frontal blow, the cleave too; only an `overwhelms` blow (the bash, the plunge) or a blow from behind gets past, and he turns slowly), siege engineer (lobs `FirePot`s that leave `BurningGround`, unblockable fire), and the Captain.
- Encounters are designed, not scattered: each fight in a level asks its own question (see PROGRESS.md). Level data gives soldiers an `activity` (an animation they loop until they notice the hero: `loot`, `burn`, `menace`, `chat`, `watch`, `execute`, `stab`), `hidden` (out of sight until their group wakes), `guard` (block chance), `group`, `patrol`, and for an executioner `victim` (a captive's id) and `delay`. A blow on a soldier who has not noticed the hero kills him (the Captain's armour excepted); a sprung ambusher chases the hero however far he goes; one who hears the hero behind him is startled a moment before he turns; one who notices shouts and comrades within 210 px join (not a headsman, who finishes his work). A headsman strikes `delay` seconds after the hero comes within 300 px unless killed or turned first; saved and lost captives are remembered (`saved_<id>`, `lost_<id>`).
- Gore is presentation: the body only decides what a killing blow cut off (`MongolSoldier.severed`, from the attack's `severs`/`sever_chance`, head for surprise kills and ripostes) and falls in that animation; `shared/vfx/gore_director.gd` (wired in `app/main.gd`) throws the pieces (`GorePiece`), pumps the wound (`BloodFountain`, following the wound frame by frame from the soldier's `GoreSet`), pools and splats the blood. The gore setting (full/reduced) is `GameSettings.gore` → `MongolSoldier.dismemberment` and `GoreDirector.full`. What is and is not shown: `docs/art_style_guide.md` §10.
- Fairness rules (tested): every enemy attack telegraphs at least 0.22 s before it lands (white: block or parry; amber, `AttackDefinition.low`: a sweep no standing guard stops, jump or roll; red: unblockable, roll), except a chain's follow-up (`chained`); a guarding soldier turns to a hero behind him only after `EnemyBrain.GUARD_TURN`; the hero is untouchable through a finisher; at most two soldiers attack at once (`EnemyBrain.MAX_ATTACKERS`); no soldier starts an attack on a hero who is invulnerable or was hit within `EnemyBrain.BREATHER`; the hero's post-hit invulnerability (0.9 s) outlasts his stagger, and a roll breaks a stagger after 0.18 s; soldiers never walk off ledges.

## Architecture in one breath
Input or AI → `Warrior` / `MongolSoldier` state machines → `Combatant.begin_attack` → animation frames open the `Hitbox` with the blade sweep → `Hurtbox` → `Combatant.receive_hit` (`judge_hit`: HIT, BLOCKED, PARRIED, DODGED, GUARD_BROKEN) → `on_struck` / `on_hit_landed` → signals → `app/main.gd` → VFX, sound, camera shake, hit-stop, HUD. The session (`AbbasidGame`) loads a `Level` into `World`, inserts the hero after the level's `Enemies` node, binds the camera and HUD, and handles lamps (`Checkpoint`), pages (`Manuscript`), people (`Npc` + `DialogueLibrary`; always spoken to: until their `requires` flag is set they say their `<dialogue>_waiting` words and nothing is remembered; `Npc.gives_flag` hands something over), captives (`Captive`), captives under a headsman's sabre (`Level.captive_killed`, `captive_saved`), triggers (`StoryTrigger`: hints, lines, and the events `refugees` (one is shot as he runs), `ambush` (wakes `group`), `alarm` (turns `group` to the hero), `boss`), groups (`Level.group_cleared` sets `<group>_cleared`) and the exit (`LevelExit`; `Level.next_level` and `exit_card` lead on through a `StoryCard`, the last level to the ending). The story is data: each level's `objectives` (`flag|KEY`, first match wins) come from `tools/levels/<level>.mjs`; `main.gd` holds no level-specific story. A boss fight is a `BossArena` (barriers, camera bounds, the boss) started by a `boss` trigger; `CaptainBrain` runs the Captain; the blow that would kill him beats him to one knee (`MongolSoldier.beaten`) and the session plays the finishing stroke (`_on_boss_beaten`, `Warrior.cinematic_strike`, `MongolSoldier.finish`). Archers left alone in a group flee, so a group fight always ends where the hero can see it. The hero's moves: the light combo (three cuts), the heavy cleave (breaks guards), block and parry, the shield bash (heavy with the shield up), the roll; in the air the slash (`profile.air_slashes` a jump) and the plunge (`Warrior.State.PLUNGE`: the blade turned down, a drop at `plunge_speed`, then `plunge_landing`); down and jump drop him through one-way planks; the light button late in a roll is the rolling cut (it chains into the thrust); `throw` flicks a knife (`profile.max_knives`, refilled at lamps). What he can do is what he has learned (`Warrior.techniques`, `knows()`): the session sets it on entering a level from `Level.known_techniques` (what the levels before taught) and the `knows_<technique>` flags; a page of a treatise (`Manuscript.teaches`) or a gift (`Npc.teaches`) teaches one (`AbbasidGame._learn`): the bash in the Streets of Ash, the knives from Salim, the plunge in the Scholars' Quarter, the rolling cut at the Last Gate. Finishers: a staggered soldier at half health or less glows red and the heavy button plays one of the `Warrior.finishers` (`FinisherDefinition`, generated in `assets/characters/warrior/finishers/`): the hero's `finish_*` and the soldier's `finished_*` frame-locked, the soldier set `distance` px ahead, the hero untouchable; the full one (bars, slow time in `main.gd`) when no other soldier near is in the fight (`Combatant.in_fight`, `MongolSoldier.engaged` set by his brain), otherwise quick (`Warrior.QUICK_FINISHER`); each gives back `profile.finisher_stamina`. Progress is a `SaveGame` (`user://save.cfg`); settings a `GameSettings` (`user://settings.cfg`); the `ABBASID_USER_PREFIX` environment variable (set by `tools/run_godot_cli.mjs`) keeps checks away from the player's files.

## Tools
- Godot 4.7.2: `D:\SteamLibrary\steamapps\common\Godot Engine\godot.windows.opt.tools.64.exe` (Compatibility renderer). The Coding-Solo Godot MCP can run, read debug output and stop the game.
- Node.js runs every asset generator (`tools/asset_generation/`, `tools/levels/`, `tools/write_main_scene.mjs`) and the Godot runner `tools/run_godot_cli.mjs` (fails on script errors, times out). No Python packages are needed.
- Tests: `./tools/run_tests.ps1` (headless suites: gameplay incl. real key/pad input, enemies, session play-through, level traversal autoplayer). `-Visual` renders `captures/level/` and `captures/session/`.

## Art pipeline
- Characters are 3D models rendered into pixel art, all in Node (no Blender): `lib/meshes.mjs` (lofted tubes, ellipsoids, lathes, shields, ribbons), `characters/body3d.mjs` (the skeleton: hips, twisting torso, head, two-bone IK legs and arms, a centre-gripped shield, blade direction; skinning; cloth chains simulated through each animation), `characters/figure3d.mjs` (shared parts: torso, bands, a skirt that follows the thighs, sleeves, trousers, boots), `lib/raster.mjs` (a G-buffer at 4 samples a pixel, camera turned 22° to a three-quarter view) and `lib/sprite_shader.mjs` (majority downsampling, palette ramps by light, separation lines, warm back rim, outline). Models: `yusuf.mjs`, `mongol3d.mjs` (swordsman, spearman, archer, captain at 1.14×, veteran, maceman at 1.06×, shieldbearer with a `towerBoard` shield, engineer with clay pots; a blade can be `hidden`: its line is only for the hitboxes, as the mace head's), `townsfolk3d.mjs`; animations: `yusuf_animations.mjs`, `mongol3d_animations.mjs`, `townsfolk3d_animations.mjs`, helpers in `pose3d.mjs`. A finisher's two halves share one timing table (`characters/finisher_timing.mjs`: frame durations, distance, cut/burst/death frames), which also writes its `FinisherDefinition`. `render3d.mjs` draws blades as two-pixel lines (hidden behind nearer parts) and exports blade hilt/tip per frame for the hitboxes; sword trails are not in the sprites but drawn at runtime by `features/combat/sword_trail.gd` from those blade positions. `lib/root_motion.mjs` holds planted feet still while an attack's lunge (read from its `AttackDefinition`) carries the body. Every canvas is 128 px tall with the feet 4 px above its bottom (sprite offset (0, -60)); widths 96–208 by action. A pose can `hide` parts (a sabre put away to loot) and `show` optional ones (the codex a soldier throws on the pyre, a wound's raw cap). An animation's `wound` (frames => point) is exported per frame; `renderPiece` draws a cut-off piece tumbling through turns. Each soldier (and each townsperson who can be executed) gets a GoreSet (`<prefix>_gore.tres`: pieces' SpriteFrames, their resting heights and origins, the pieces each cut throws, the wounds); the dead of the streets are props (`CORPSES` in `townsfolk3d_animations.mjs`, drawn by `environment/props3d.mjs`).
- Character art follows `docs/art_style_guide.md` (scale, light, value groups, faction colours, silhouettes, animation and effect rules). Check animations with `node tools/animation_lint.mjs` (pops, foot sliding, loop seams, the ground speeds `walk_animation_speed`/`run_animation_speed` must use), the cast in each level with `tests/capture_cast.gd`, and their timing in `captures/animation_reel.html` (`node tools/review_reel.mjs`).
- Distant layers come from the user's concept paintings: `environment/painted.mjs` resamples a crop of a painting (named by the level's `painting` entry) to the game's grid and reduces it to a palette (`lib/quantize.mjs`); the level's street backdrop is then mapped to that palette, built lower and darker so the painted city shows above it. The camera sits 84 px above the hero's feet so the city fills the upper half of the frame. `features/presentation/post_process.gdshader` (layer 5, over the world, under the HUD) adds warm bloom, split toning and a vignette; the fighters' shader (`features/combat/hit_flash.gdshader`) lifts them above the scene and adds a firelit rim. No ordered dithering anywhere: gradients are smooth. Review with `tests/capture_tour.gd` (every level through the real game view).
- Environment painters (for what the paintings do not cover): `environment/layers.mjs` (sky, skyline, city, river; one `LOOKS` entry per hour: night, smoke, predawn, dawn), `backdrop.mjs` (the street-side facades by section theme: houses, ruin, hammam, mosque, khan, potters/spices/books, darb, portal, madrasa, library, river, rampart, gatehouse, camp), `facades.mjs` (over solid blocks), `props.mjs` and `tiles.mjs` (shared by every level, in `assets/environments/market/`). Set pieces the soldiers handle are modelled in 3D and rendered with the characters' rasteriser and shader (`props3d.mjs`: the book pyre, the looted chest), so they match the figures. `build_environment.mjs --level <name>` builds one level's art into its `env` folder; `--shared` builds tiles and props. All colours come from `lib/palette.mjs` ramps. Inspect `captures/art_review/` and in-engine captures before calling art done.
